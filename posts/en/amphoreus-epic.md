# Amphoreus: The Golden Epic

## The Wager

When Talanton, Titan of Law, fell on the Day of Ghosts, Zagreus, Titan of Trickery, seized the opening. It took divine authority by force and proclaimed its law to the world: "gambling."

A king wishes to expand his realm? Put his power and territory on the table!
A mortal desires divine authority? Go throw dice against a god!

Thus every mountain and river, every city and citizen of Amphoreus became a stake. In a single night, nobles beyond counting fell while nameless souls began their ascent.

The followers of Zagreus became the god's agents, impartial and absolutely neutral. Supreme among them stood the demigod called the "Wind of Dolos"—Cifera.

Legend holds that Cifera once wagered against Kephale and won everything the Titan carried upon its back. Thereafter, Okhema became the greatest gambling house in the world. Countless gamblers climbed the Cloudedge with hope in their hearts, seeking fine gold and jade or whole territories to rule. All returned disappointed.

Cifera always has the last laugh—the people accepted those words as truth.

---

One day, a ragged little girl with golden hair knocked on the doors of the Dawncloud Cliff casino.

"Cifera, wager with me."

Though destitute, the girl carried herself with uncommon dignity.

"My name is Aglaea, last child of Okhema's old nobility. I have come to take back everything that is mine."

A breeze stirred. Aglaea knew a curious cat had pricked up her ears. She pointed toward the bathing pool beside her.

"The water here never stops flowing. Let us wager whether a golden laurel leaf will pass through that outlet within thirty seconds... I say it will."

Another breeze lifted the hem of Aglaea's tattered skirt, carrying a playful laugh: "Then I say it will not."

Neither had named a stake. Cifera had not even shown herself. Yet the wager had begun. Onlookers throughout the bath held their breath as Aglaea slowly lowered herself beside the water, as if the outcome were already known.

A golden laurel leaf appeared in the pool above the outlet. It spun with the current, almost hesitant. Suddenly, wind rippled the water. Just as the leaf was about to fall through, it vanished before every watching eye. At the same instant, Cifera appeared before Aglaea wearing a teasing smile.

"...Three seconds... two... one..."

Aglaea removed the only precious thing she wore—the golden laurel leaf in her hair—and let the current sweep it through the outlet.

"Zero. I win." Her expression did not change.

"What use is winning a wager with no stakes?" Cifera did not even bother to point out the girl's "cheating."

"At least you appeared."

"Speak, little girl with nothing to your name. What could you possibly wager with me?"

"I am not without anything. I can stake *myself*. And what I want... is *you*. Fair enough, wouldn't you say?"

## Hero of the Sky

A thousand years of war against the Titans left the world in ruin.
A century of the Chrysos War left every nation exhausted and divided.

Then a spear surging with mortal fury pierced the smoke and flame that had buried the prophecy of salvation.

Aquila, shaper of the firmament: the hundred-eyed divine bird, thunder in its talons and a blazing sun upon its brow. Though Nikador had blinded ninety-nine of its eyes, the last remained, glaring at the children of dawn and dusk who had received its blessing only to raise weapons against it.

The Chrysos Heir of the sky, Seliose the Sun-Thunder Knight, donned her wings at dusk and entered the forbidden ground of the Eye of Dawn and Dusk.

At her left strode Solabis, lion-headed Wing of the Blazing Sun, breathing divine fire hot enough to scorch the world.
At her right danced Lunabis, horse-bodied Plume of Star and Moon, whose circling hooves struck constellations across the sky.

One warrior and two beasts challenged the sovereign of heaven, vowing to slay the god and seize its Coreflame.

Every roar of the Titan gave birth to lightning; every tilt of its body summoned a hurricane.
Its wounded blood fell as boiling rain and gathered into scalding floods across the earth.

At the battle's end, the Wing of the Blazing Sun set Aquila's feathers aflame while the Plume of Star and Moon bound its divine body.
Her own wings broken and her body covered in wounds, the hero of the sky struggled to lift her spear and pierced that final, terror-casting eye.

The Titan's body crashed to earth. At the same time, the heavens tore like brocade, and the sky began to fall with its god.

The terror and prayers of mortals brought the hero to her feet once more.
Seliose, Sun-Thunder Knight and the first Chrysos Heir to slay a god, charged with her two valiant winged companions into Aquila's disintegrating body.

She became one with the Titan and raised the firmament again, leaving the world only this fierce promise:

> My descendants shall inherit the blessing. They will cross the rainbow bridge and climb into the heavens once more. At the end of that divine realm of creation, they shall take this Coreflame from my hand.

## Farewell and the Lie

That girl Cifera fooled all of Amphoreus with one magnificent lie—fooled every last one of you into believing the great sphere on Kephale's back would illuminate Okhema forever.

She even deceived that foolish trial of succession and allowed the great Zagreus... to live!

You guessed correctly. Cifera is dead. Every one of her lies has now scattered into ash.

All I sought throughout my life was to weave the greatest trick in the world together with Cipher! Suppose the future held one hundred million possibilities: by any reckoning, ninety-nine million, nine hundred ninety-nine thousand, nine hundred ninety-nine of them ended in destruction.

But with this exquisite deception, we fooled every idiot, villain, and calamity in the world and made that one-in-a-hundred-million possibility real...

This is the miracle I spent my entire life pursuing—the miracle for which I was willing to become what you see before you!

You are Amphoreus's unsung hero, Cifera. You saved us all.

You sold out the world... and bought mortals another thousand years of life.
