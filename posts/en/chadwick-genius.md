# Genius Society #79: Calderon Chadwick

## First Meeting: The Old Man in a Dream

The old man before you gazed calmly toward the City of Dreams in the distance.

Following his eyes, you saw smoke rising above the city's fabricated horizon—the sign that dreamchasers had begun their revelry for the countless time. The clamor of the Planet of Festivities felt infinitely far away. Neither you nor the old man had anything to do with them.

He sat like a statue, with scarcely a sign of breath. Watching him, you held your own without realizing it—then discovered, to your surprise, that people in dreams did not need to breathe.

"Hello, young Nameless."

"How did you know?"

The old man's gaze fell upon your left shoulder. "I have forgotten many things... but I still remember that emblem. The Nameless are a company of legends. No one has spoken with me in a very long time. Far... too long. My name is Chadwick. Will you tell me yours, child?"

"I am Stelle."

"Stelle... an unusual name. Once, a name alone was enough for me to guess where someone came from. But you... you are different, child. Even through the heavy filter of this dream, I can feel the vitality surging within you. You cannot stop; at every moment you seek the road of adventure—or rather, of 'Trailblaze.'"

He paused. "Something in you fills me with curiosity and joy. I have not felt either emotion for a long time—after all, I now know nothing of myself. Thank you for coming before me."

Unfortunately, the Bloodhounds soon arrived: the Family's watchers. Chadwick helped me out of the situation, but before I left he said, "I would dearly like to speak with you longer, child, but you cannot remain here. The Bloodhound Family never warns the same person twice."

## Investigation: Clockwork and Fragments of Memory

(After the Bloodhounds left, I returned to Chadwick.)

I used Clockwork on him. It unquestionably succeeded, yet Chadwick's expression did not change. Still, I could sense movement beneath the surface. Long years had taught the old man not to display emotion, but rage and joy too powerful to suppress still made his limbs tremble.

"I... seem to remember something. Something is beginning to rise. My memories remain broken; many fragments are incomplete... but I can truly see them now."

His limbs stopped shaking with anger. He closed his eyes and began to trace the course of his life.

Countless scattered fragments flashed before him. He tried to catch them in a net, but they slipped slyly through its holes and vanished into the ocean of the unconscious. Only a handful remained.

"I see... something. Some kind of... weapon, followed by endless slaughter and death. It seems to be connected to me. Why...? I do not think I was ever a man who loved violence..."

"I see... people, too. Ah... extraordinary people, shining even within these scattered memories. I... I was one of them. I was... a member of the Genius Society. I finally remember—I belonged to the Genius Society."

But why was he here? Why had he allowed time to pass without ever trying to save himself?

"The Family... They must have exerted some influence upon me, one I could not resist. I cannot escape it alone. Some powerful external force must intervene... perhaps... the Genius Society."

"As it happens, I know several geniuses," I said.

"You would help an old man you met by chance, child, even without knowing what kind of man he is?"

"I do not think you are a bad person."

He smiled. "I understand... You are driven by trust in your own intuition. Thank you, child. This dream is beautiful, and it is false. If I remain, I will never save myself. You have given me one thread of hope. That is enough... Go now, before the Bloodhounds return. I will carry that hope and wait for you."

## Joining Forces: Herta and Screwllum

(I sent Herta a message.)

"I need to ask you something. Do you know a man named Chadwick?"

"Chadwick? That's an ordinary name. There must be tens of billions of people with it across the universe. Can't you be more specific?"

"I met an old man named Chadwick in Penacony's dreamscape. He said he used to be a member of the Genius Society."

"??? I do not have time for jokes. Stop wasting it. You are joking, right?"

"...So you are serious. Come back to the space station. Now. And keep your mouth shut—do not tell anyone else. I am not joking either."

(When I returned to the station, Screwllum was there too.)

Screwllum said, "I met Dr. Chadwick on two occasions. The first was in the vigor of his youth; the second, several decades later. His circumstances were entirely different. Dr. Chadwick asked me for help when he was approximately fifty years of age. I agreed to his request. Thereafter, Calderon Chadwick disappeared without word, as though the sea of stars bore no trace that he had ever existed."

Herta added, "Calderon Chadwick—Genius Society #79. His specialties included solid-state physics, applied Imaginary theory, and orbital mechanics."

"His number is higher than yours?" I asked.

"...Seat number is not status. Trying to provoke me with that? Not a chance."

Screwllum continued, "Since the flame of life first ignited within my core, I have encountered very few souls truly worthy of the word 'genius' on my long journey through the stars. Madam Herta beside me is one. Calderon Chadwick is another."

Herta snorted. "Thanks for slipping in the compliment."

Before we left, Screwllum told me one more thing. "Calderon Chadwick's greatest achievement was a weapon called the 'Imaginary Implosion Pulse.' That weapon once took the homes and lives of innumerable beings."

> Note: The Imaginary Implosion Pulse is a weapon that causes a directed collapse of Imaginary energy, powerful enough to destroy a planetary civilization. Dr. Chadwick spent the rest of his life atoning for its creation.

## Confrontation: The Family's Secret

Screwllum and I returned to The Reverie in Penacony. After Screwllum introduced himself, the receptionist Ellie's manner changed subtly. His identity plainly forced the Family to treat us with care.

But when we mentioned Chadwick, Ellie refused us. "Dr. Chadwick is not available to receive visitors."

Unhurried, Screwllum posed a question. "To my knowledge, Calderon Chadwick does not belong to a long-life species, and my final conversation with him occurred two Amber Eras ago. May I ask what means the Family has used to prolong his life, whether said means received approval from interstellar medical authorities, and whether it complies with Penacony's own regulations?"

Ellie had no answer.

Screwllum continued. "Then may I conclude that, for the sake of your principles, you would rather place the entire Family in opposition to Planet Screwllum? And can you bear the consequences of making an enemy of the Genius Society?"

(So Screwllum has this side to him...)

At last, the head of the Family ordered them to let us pass.

## The Truth: The Old Man in the Memory Bubble

Chadwick's room was empty. Only the breathing, trembling bubbles inside the Dreampool showed that time still flowed.

Screwllum slowly lowered his right hand into the pool and carefully lifted out a crystalline bubble. The moment it left the liquid, it began to float upward, stopping in midair.

"At last we meet again, Dr. Calderon Chadwick."

"This bubble... is Chadwick?"

Screwllum explained, "As a member of a short-life species, the probability that Calderon Chadwick's physical body has survived to the present approaches zero. But Penacony's Family understands memoria and its related technologies deeply. Even if they could not preserve his body, they could extract the foundational substance of his brain and allow his thoughts to continue 'living.' In one sense, this is immortality—though many would argue it is not 'life' at all."

Screwllum gave me a pill to carry into the dream and deliver to Chadwick.

> Note: This "selective blocking drug," created by another Genius Society member, can shield parts of a person's memory and knowledge. Chadwick once used it to prevent the IPC from extracting the Implosion Pulse technology from his mind.

## Memory: Twenty Years in the Laboratory

I entered the dream again and gave Chadwick the pill. After swallowing it, he began to relive his entire life—

**Scene One: The Laboratory Is Born**

A young Chadwick stood upon an abandoned planet. Dudley of the Technology Department told him, "From this place, we—the Technology Department and your team—will create the galaxy's future together."

Chadwick hesitated. "Honestly, Dud... I am concerned. What you want—that 'weapon' which exists only in theory... it truly could overturn the galaxy."

Dudley smiled. "Of course, Chad. Do not underestimate the Technology Department. We may not possess your brilliant mind, but we certainly do not lack 'vision.'"

He told Chadwick their work would oppose the coming threat of Destruction. "I will not say 'our cause is just.' I know you are not that naive, Chad."

**Scene Two: The Kitchen Behind the Miracle**

The laboratory doors opened. Dudley told the team, "Crossing this threshold means choosing a second life. For a long time to come, this laboratory will be the whole of your existence. Here you will work, research, relax, and confide in one another. You will be cut off from the world until the project succeeds or is declared a failure."

No one left.

"(Here, I can move the world.)"

(We studied, researched, and worked without regard for day or night. They rarely questioned me. They trusted me.)

**Scene Three: Impasse and Breakthrough**

Experiment thirty-seven failed. Chadwick made a daring decision: disable the safety protocols and increase the collision rate to 1.3 times the default threshold. His colleagues feared for their safety, but Chadwick said, "Over the last three years, we have overcome almost every theoretical obstacle. But if we cannot build the Imaginary Implosion Pulse... every effort will be lost. The IPC will not allow us to take a single result from this place. Not one page. As physicists, we have no choice but to succeed."

> Note: From this moment, Chadwick was trapped in the cycle of crossing one boundary after another to prove his worth. Events later showed that he truly had no choice—but "having no choice" was itself a predicament designed for him.

(Once the impasse was broken, the experiments went extraordinarily well. The laboratory even received a visitor...)

Screwllum visited to observe a test detonation. In private, he asked Chadwick, "Why did you agree to help develop a weapon such as the Imaginary Implosion Pulse?"

Chadwick answered, "Because Dudley... said this weapon could strike the forces of Destruction. We are doing the right thing."

Screwllum was silent before offering a warning. "I believe each of us has the right to choose a way of life. We may discard the 'redundant thoughts' that leave us lost and fix our eyes only upon what stands before us and the fields in which we excel. But take care, Doctor. For people such as you and me, a moment's negligence may bring grief to myriad lives. Intelligence is the universe's blessing to us—and the burden it imposes upon us."

At the end of his life, Chadwick admitted that Screwllum had seen through his self-deception even then.

**Scene Four: Betrayal**

At last, the day arrived.

Dudley tore away the mask. "I regret this, Chad. In your own field, you are a 'genius,' yet you understand nothing of the structure of power. You are too naive to see the whole game—you do not even understand the age in which you live. You should have realized that both of us are pieces on this board. Once a piece is placed, it no longer chooses where it moves."

"A 'weapon' is created to strike an enemy. Is that principle difficult to understand?"

Chadwick finally understood. "The past twenty years—everything that happened here—was only an experiment. Nothing I built with my own hands ever belonged to us."

"Stop deceiving yourself, Chad. From the beginning, you knew what filthy water you had entered. You merely chose not to look."

The system counted down on the firing platform. The control-room operator refused to press the button. "It was you, wasn't it? You sympathize with Dr. Chadwick?" Dudley wasted no more words and ordered soldiers to force the launch.

> Note: In the game, the player and Trailblazer choose to "refuse to press the detonation button." No matter what you select, the ending is the same: the weapon fires. But those three refusals are the accusation Chadwick least wants to face within his memory.

Five... four... three... two... one... zero—

The Imaginary Implosion Pulse detonated.

That day, twenty-four celestial bodies of satellite class or larger were destroyed, including three sub-planets bearing civilizations of Class D or above.

## Flight: Selective Forgetting

"How many people did the Imaginary Pulse kill? ...A number I exhausted every effort trying to forget. After that day, I never dared glance into a mirror. I feared I would see a demon—a murderer who stole countless lives and homes."

Chadwick dismissed his team, abandoned the laboratory, took the crucial data, and fled. He sought Screwllum but refused sanctuary on Planet Screwllum. "No. I swore never again to involve someone who had nothing to do with this."

He asked for a different path: the selective blocking drug, capable of sealing away memory and knowledge.

"This carries the smallest cost. So long as I can temporarily forget that knowledge, the IPC will extract nothing from my mind. Whatever I must suffer... I deserve it. A person must answer for his actions; bearing the cost is a necessary part of that."

Screwllum helped him. But he also said, "At last, you can face yourself honestly, Doctor. For that alone, I am relieved. I sincerely hope you endure the difficult time ahead."

## The Prison: Two Amber Eras

The drug worked better than imagined. It did not merely block his knowledge; it blocked his ability to relearn it.

The IPC did not surrender. When Chadwick grew old, they found him on Penacony. Using Garden of Recollection technology, they extracted the memoria from his brain, sealed it in a memory bubble, and kept it active by connecting it to the dreamscape.

Under the Family's watch, Chadwick wandered the dream for two Amber Eras. Day after day, he sat upon a bench in the City of Dreams and watched dreamchasers celebrate along the horizon. People came and went. No one knew him, and no one could speak with him.

"At first, they pretended to be courteous. They tempted me with possessions and tried to trade material comfort and promises for my knowledge... It had no effect. Later they lost patience and forced me to read papers on applied Imaginary theory. But the blocking drug worked far better than expected: it sealed not only the knowledge in my mind but also my ability to acquire related knowledge. Eventually they accepted that I had become a useless old man. From a certain point onward, their strategy was simply to wait for my death."

"I am afraid, young one... I fear the defenses I built will collapse entirely at the moment death arrives. If this continues, the IPC will eventually recover and monopolize the Imaginary Pulse. All my efforts will have been for nothing..."

## The End: A Final Trust

Screwllum appeared before him. "Your efforts were not wasted. Even after the death of your body, you guarded that knowledge and offered no opening to those with malicious intent—not through the drug's effect, but through persistence engraved at the foundation of your consciousness."

"Good... Nothing could be better."

At last, Chadwick said the words he had held back for most of his life:

"I did not join that experiment for any grand principle. I did it for myself. I wanted the world to see my talent and my field. Nothing more."

> Reading this, I found it more moving than any grand narrative. He was neither hero nor saint—only a man who wanted his talent to be seen. For that one desire, he spent two Amber Eras atoning.

Screwllum said, "The rationality of organic life is exceedingly difficult to comprehend. Yet the impulses of organic life often contain magnificent and romantic poetry. I am shaken by it."

In the last moment of his life, Chadwick entrusted all his knowledge to Herta.

"How infuriating. He was only an ordinary person with a lifespan of a few decades, yet he achieved something this astonishing." Despite her words, Herta accepted his trust.

Tens of thousands of formulas and equations flashed through her mind. She saw a message he had left:

> "To Madam Herta: I held my position throughout my lifetime. Yet with the IPC's resources, one day they will find the technology and method to reproduce the Imaginary Implosion Pulse. What is done cannot be undone. From the instant the Imaginary Pulse was born, every corner of the universe became destined to guard against its threat. I cannot reverse time, but I can still do one thing: entrust my knowledge and technology to someone worthy, laying the foundation for a system of deterrence capable of checking the IPC's power. My life has reached its end. But from this moment onward, I wish to become a sharp sword forever suspended above tyranny and villainy."

Screwllum said, "My calculations once showed that Calderon Chadwick's research held the potential to save hundreds of millions of lives. It now appears highly probable that this result will come to pass."

Herta was silent for a moment. "I never expected him to entrust his life's work to me... Did he not know this station cooperates with the IPC? Still, his final wish... must be respected. Then we will find a way around the IPC's eyes. On my name as a genius, I will accomplish this for Dr. Calderon Chadwick."

## Afterword

Once, in an age when worlds were isolated from one another, some civilizations already possessed weapons capable of destroying themselves. Fear of that power drove them to build complex rules and laws, striving to keep the switch of self-destruction from the hands of madmen and thugs.

Later, winding starways linked the sea of stars, and technology and resources began to flow between worlds. New weapons tens of thousands of times stronger emerged one after another.

Yet strangely, as the weapons grew more powerful, the intricate rules meant to prevent their abuse were simplified. When the blade's point turned outward instead of inward, people everywhere became more tolerant of violence.

Suppose that upon some distant world, myriad lives are consumed by flame and cries of agony fill the sky. To you and me beneath a tree, gazing upward, it would seem no more than someone plucking one star from the countless lights above.

"Intelligence"—what a bewildering word. Some believe it the answer to every question and the final point of every virtue. Yet how many people of extraordinary intellect throughout the galaxy suffer endless worldly troubles? Whether geniuses are too proud or ordinary people too envious, no one can say. Only one point lies beyond dispute: intelligence can coexist with selfish desire.

May you find eternal peace within that sea of trees, Doctor.
