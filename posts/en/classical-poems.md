# Two Classical Chinese Poems

## To the Tune of Tangduoling — Liu Guo

Reeds cover the river isle; cold sands edge a shallow stream.
Twenty years on, I pass the Southern Tower again.
My boat beneath the willows is scarcely moored—
how many days remain
until Mid-Autumn comes?

The Yellow Crane Tower is gone, its rocky bank broken.
Have old friends ever returned?
The same old rivers and hills are steeped in fresh sorrow.
I would buy osmanthus blossoms and share the wine,
yet it can never be
that youthful journey again.

## Plum Blossom Prelude: Snowbound at Jingxi — Jiang Jie

A white gull asks why my lone boat lies at anchor:
is it my body that stays, or my heart?
If my heart would stay, why then are my brows locked in sorrow?
The wind beats the little curtain; lamplight whirls.
Facing my idle shadow, alone in the cold,
I remember journeys long past.

Those old journeys—where are they now?
The tower beyond the blossoms, the boat beneath the willows.
I dream and dream, yet dreams cannot reach them;
the cold river flows in vain.
Endless yellow clouds soak through my cotton coat.
They say no one bears sorrow quite like mine:
in tonight's snow,
the plum blossoms
seem to share my grief.
