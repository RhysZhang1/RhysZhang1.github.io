# Ode to Youth

## The Swordsman

Eastward went the youth; on a desolate road he met a savage ape.
A spring branch became his blade; he drove it back and stilled the people's anger.
Westward went the man in his prime; frogs called beneath the gallery eaves.
One branch astonished every guest, its arc arrayed among the stars.
Northward went the seasoned warrior; a monstrous hound devoured the countryside.
Red leaves wrapped its bloodied fangs and became his newest sword.
Southward went the aging man; a swarm of locusts fell upon his home.
One sword held the pass; when snow had cleared, a thread of life remained.

White at the temples, he sighed and turned for home; the markets passed his legend on.
He hid his blade and sought a quiet life, drifting with the crowd to a grand feast.
Then among the honored guests he saw a robe recalling that ape of old,
lips brushing cunning teeth, a voice awarding hollow fame.
His withered hand shook out his sleeve; white hair bristled—he would not join their stream.
Through mud he strode into the courtyard, broke off a branch, and was young once more.

## Chasing the Dream

May you pursue your dream for thirty thousand miles; may your sword stand against a million.
May you draw your carved bow full as the moon and shoot the celestial wolf in the northwest.
Time and power may change, yet the dynasty endures.
Fight for me for one day; within my heart, you will be king forever.

Three years or five are nothing—what matter victory and defeat?
You raise the sky as a pillar of jade; I span the boundless sea with a beam of violet gold.
What is there to fear? I see you:
a sleeping dragon rising through the clouds, borne a million miles upon the wind in a breath.

Three years to hone one sword. I long to see you
leading yellow hound and bearing goshawk, crossing mountains, cleaving mighty rivers;
reining your horse with golden spear held level, cracking your whip to the songs of Qin.
Say nothing—only watch that gallant youth,
riding the four seas, mastering the eight wilds.
High heaven and broad ocean give the fish room to leap; he chases wind and sun and races the phoenix.

Beyond the Northern Sea, across the southern frontier, the young rider roams in every direction.
Who is he? Where does he go?
Heaven towers ten thousand feet like stairs before him. Do not worry, do not fear, do not look back.
Wait until the silver dragon shakes itself awake again—and roars at the sky!

Heaven cracks and earth splits as the dragon lashes its tail; mountains fall and seas rise as the phoenix lifts its brow.
The dynasty neither dies nor yields; the sleeping dragon rides the wind a million miles.
The crown of former days is worn no more. Raise your hand—where is your sword?
Seven thousand in white robes break the enemy stronghold; the three armies spend their lives, and I command!

Burn the boats: Qin belonged to Chu. Sleep on brushwood, taste gall: Yue swallowed Wu.
Arms sharpened and horses fed, the young warrior takes thirteen passes, cutting generals down and seizing their standards.
Here stands a great commander, a spirit the world can scarcely find.
Three feet of tempered steel shake all beneath heaven; when he mounts the terrace to receive command, five continents stare in wonder.

Seven hundred peaks of Wushan roar like thunder; the Ba River coils three times like a wandering dragon.
Young and old across the mountain city rush outside. Why? Wherever he goes, they drink the wine of triumph.
Through storm at night, the waves rage. Looking back, a thousand-foot leviathan plays in the river.
The Kunpeng spreads wings across eighty thousand miles; suddenly bright, the rising sun breaks through a sky of cloud.

Wind and thunder stir at his fingertips; ten thousand miles call him to the road.
I was born a proud and untamed youth and never asked which way was east or west.
No legend exists until it is made; why wait for a mighty wind before cleaving waves?
Young and fearless, spend your sweat where you stand; tomorrow, gain the summit.
Mountains and rivers change, sun and moon renew, hot blood surges—
without a last stand at the river's edge, how could victory arrive at the first charge?

It is a reed crossing the river, a royal elephant fording the stream;
an antelope hanging its horns, leaving no trail in snow; light steps upon the waves, silk stockings raising dust.
Like an eagle traversing the vault of heaven, or a flood dragon rising from the sea.
No—none of these! Look closely: what magnificent lightness skill this young hero has!
As the saying goes: ten thousand miles I walk alone. There is no need to see me off!

Young Qin Qiong battles Lord Guan; now watch the Flying General face the Hegemon-King.
Do not call a mere boy too reckless—the young should be fierce as tiger and wolf.
He bends the earth until it breaks and tramples sun and moon; he pierces heaven and makes the sky his tent.
What a splendid young man!
If one day he gains a treasured sword, he will shatter the Palace of Heaven to test its edge.

He travels every river, lake, and sea; tastes sour, sweet, bitter, hot, and salt;
knows wind, frost, thunder, rain, and lightning; battles through east, south, west, north, and center.
The horse is thin, the rain cape torn—young one, do not grieve.
Sing out, laugh loud, fasten the great blade; slay through spring and summer, then cut down autumn and winter.

Long years of battle forge a famous general. Fear nothing; be wild and proud!
Fearless youth—
afraid of neither fiend nor phantom; advance or retreat by your own will!
Youth without fear—
smash through every demon and specter, bold and blazing!
