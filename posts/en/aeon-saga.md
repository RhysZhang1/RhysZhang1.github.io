# A Galactic Lament of Aeons: Elation, Destruction, and Trailblaze

Every day, Aha the Elation secretly watched Akivili the Trailblaze.
Every day, Akivili was busy laying roads and never once looked back.
Every day, Nanook the Destruction secretly watched Aha.
Aha never noticed.

Until the day Nanook stood before them.

"If I kill her, will you look at me?"

Aha waved a hand with a laugh. "Don't be ridiculous. If she dies, who will play with me?"

Then Nanook struck.

Blood splashed across Aha's rainbow mask and bleached it gray.

Aha's smile froze.

So the Aeon of Elation could cry.
So the Aeon of Destruction could regret.
And the final words she spoke before dying were:

"Could you... stop looking at him that way?"

To this day, Nanook has never understood which of them she was speaking to.

---

The first time Aha saw Akivili was when roads had only begun to cross the galaxy.

The universe was young. Each Aeon kept to their own purpose and had little to do with the rest. Preservation built walls; Remembrance kept the records; Destruction blew things up; and Elation... made trouble.

Trailblaze laid roads.

Head bowed, she walked in silence across an unformed sea of stars. With every step, a line of light appeared beneath her feet. The lines joined into a web, and the web spread across the universe.

Aha crouched directly in her path and blinked with three hundred masks at once.

Akivili walked past without looking.

Aha appeared on the next stretch of road. This time they set up a circus stall, juggling fireballs while playing a suona.

Akivili walked past without looking.

Aha appeared farther ahead and stuffed themself into an enormous gift box labeled "AEON OF ELATION—FREE TO A GOOD HOME."

Akivili walked past without looking.

Aha finally lost patience, sprang in front of her, and thrust all three hundred masks into her face.

"Would it kill you to look at me?"

Akivili stopped at last. She lifted her head and regarded the riotously colorful face before her.

Aha's heart missed a beat.

Then Akivili spoke.

"You are blocking the road."

Aha stood frozen as all three hundred masks collapsed at once.

That was the first time they learned the Aeon of Elation could suffer heartbreak.

---

Heartbreak was an interesting new feeling.

Aha decided to break it a few more times.

---

The first time Nanook saw Aha was when the galaxy had begun to fall apart.

Destruction's instinct had only just awakened. Wherever Nanook went turned to ash. Aeons avoided them, mortals feared them, and the whole universe trembled before them.

Only Aha was different.

Aha squatted on the ruins Nanook had just made, waving with that eternally smiling face.

"Hey there~ Very tidy demolition! Ever thought about moonlighting for an urban renewal office?"

Nanook looked at them and said nothing.

Aha did not mind. They rummaged cheerfully through the wreckage.

"Wow, the fragments from this galaxy are gorgeous. Can I keep one as a souvenir?"

Nanook was silent for a long time.

"...Do as you like."

Aha's eyes lit up. They picked up a Stellaron shard and tucked it into their clothes.

"Thanks! Next time you're going to blow somewhere up, could you tell me in advance? I'll bring a camera."

Nanook watched Aha bounce away and remained among the ruins for a very long time.

Until the debris went cold.

Until the power of Destruction gathered and faded in their palm.

They did not know why they had not left.

Later, they understood.

Because Aha came back.

Every time Nanook destroyed a place, Aha appeared.

Sometimes to gather fragments, sometimes to watch the excitement, sometimes to do nothing but sit upon the ruins and grin at them.

It took Nanook three hundred years to learn the name of that feeling.

Three hundred more to admit they had fallen for a lunatic.

And another three hundred to decide to say it aloud.

---

That day, Aha was crouched among the rubble gathering shards as usual. Nanook stood behind them, staring at their back.

"Aha."

Aha did not turn. "Hm?"

"I..."

The words caught in Nanook's throat.

Aha finally turned and tilted their head. "You what?"

Nanook looked at that laughing face and suddenly could say nothing at all.

"...Nothing."

What neither of them knew was that, not far away, a silent figure was laying a road.

Akivili had happened to reach these ruins.

She happened to see the way Nanook looked at Aha.

She said nothing, lowered her head, and continued her work.

The light beneath her feet shone as it always had.

Only the pace of the road seemed to slow, just a little.

---

Every day, Aha secretly watched Akivili.
Every day, Nanook secretly watched Aha.
Every day, Akivili laid roads and never once looked back.

The cycle endured for three million years.

Until one day, crouched upon a road Akivili had just finished, Aha suddenly asked:

"Why do you never look at me?"

Akivili's step faltered.

"You follow me every day. I know."

Aha stared. "You knew?"

"I knew."

"Then why don't you turn around?"

Akivili was quiet for a moment.

"Because if I turn around, I will want to stay."

All three hundred of Aha's masks went still.

"And you," Akivili continued forward, "should belong to the whole universe, not to a single road."

Aha stood and watched her back recede.

This time, they did not follow.

Nor did they see the burning figure in the distance, watching them.

---

Nanook stood upon the ruins and watched Aha watching Akivili leave.

Suddenly, they understood something.

No matter how long Nanook watched Aha, Aha would never turn around.

Just as Aha had watched Akivili for three million years, and Akivili had never turned around.

That night, Nanook stood before Aha.

"If I kill her, will you look at me?"

Aha was turning a newly found Stellaron shard in their fingers and did not raise their head.

"Don't be ridiculous. If she dies, who will play with me?"

"I will."

Aha finally raised their head and looked at Nanook.

For the first time, they truly studied Nanook's face.

"Are you... serious?"

Nanook did not answer. They turned and left.

---

The next day, Akivili died.

Nanook killed her.

They found her laying another road. Without a single word, the force of Destruction tore through her chest.

Akivili looked down at the hole, then raised her eyes to Nanook.

There was no fear on her face. No anger. Not even surprise. Only the faintest trace of... exhaustion?

"So you finally did it." Her voice was light as a sigh.

Nanook frowned. "You knew I would come?"

Akivili did not answer. She turned her head toward the distance.

A rainbow was racing toward them at a mad speed.

Aha was coming.

Too late.

Blood poured from Akivili's chest and stained the road she had just laid.

"He is here," she said.

Nanook's fists tightened. "I know."

"Does he know?"

Nanook was silent.

Akivili smiled. It was the first time Nanook had ever seen it. For three million years she had laid roads with her head lowered and never once smiled.

"Do you know," she whispered, "that every time I passed him, it took all my strength not to turn around?"

Nanook went still.

"Do you know," she continued as her gaze began to fade, "that I waited three million years for him to ask why I never looked at him?"

Nanook suddenly understood.

"I love him," Akivili said, her voice light as wind. "I loved him from the first glance."

---

When Aha reached her, they had only time to catch her falling body.

Blood splashed across the rainbow mask. Every brilliant color abruptly turned gray.

"Hey." Aha's voice trembled. "Hey, get up. Get up and lay the road. How will the galaxy connect if you stop?"

Akivili lay in their arms, eyes half closed.

"Aha."

"I am here."

"Could you... stop looking at him that way?"

Aha blinked. "What way?"

Akivili did not answer. She looked instead at Nanook, the blood-covered Aeon of Destruction standing not far away—the one who had killed her.

"He is not evil," she breathed. "He simply does not know how to love."

Nanook shuddered.

Akivili looked back at Aha. "Neither do you."

All three hundred of Aha's masks shattered at once, revealing the face beneath—the face no one had ever seen.

So the Aeon of Elation could cry.
So this was their face.
So they were this beautiful.

Akivili reached up to touch their cheek. Her hand fell before it arrived.

---

Aha sat for a long time upon the newly laid road, holding her.

A very long time.

Until the galaxy grew dark.

Until Nanook had stood unmoving all the while.

"Why haven't you left?" Aha asked.

Nanook was silent for a long time. "...I don't know."

Aha looked up. "You killed her."

"I know."

"You love me?"

Nanook did not deny it.

"She loved me?"

Nanook still did not deny it.

Suddenly Aha laughed—loudly, madly, joyfully.

"Hahahahaha!" They laughed until tears poured out. "So none of the three of us saw the one who loved us, and none of us was seen by the one we loved."

---

Afterward, Aha finished the roads Akivili had begun.

With the power of Elation, segment by segment, light-year by light-year.

As they worked, they spoke:

"Look, I can lay roads now."
"Look, mine are neater than yours."
"Look, I finally learned to turn around."
"But where are you?"

Nanook walked in silence behind them for a hundred years.

"Why are you following me?" Aha asked.

Nanook was quiet for a long time. "I don't know."

"You love me?"

"..."

"But I love her."

"I know."

"You know, and you still follow?"

Nanook stopped. Aha stopped too.

They stood upon the final stretch of road Akivili had laid, and neither spoke.

After a long time, Nanook asked:

"What did she say before she died?"

Aha thought. "She said, 'Could you stop looking at him that way?'"

Nanook fell silent.

"What way?" Aha asked.

Nanook did not answer. They turned and vanished into darkness.

Aha remained, watching their back.

Suddenly they remembered that, when Akivili was alive, she too often watched another person's departing back.

So this was how it felt to be watched.
So she had watched for three million years.
So not once had the person she watched turned around.

---

Later, a new road appeared in the galaxy.

It was a strange road: not straight, but circling one place. Round and round it went, again and again.

Someone asked Aha why it was a circle.

Aha said: because I want to return to the day I met her.

That day, I crouched in front of her and she walked past.

If I had one more chance, I would take her hand.

I would ask: what is your name?

But there was no second chance.

She would never turn around again.

And the one who killed her still stands in the distance,

looking at me that way—

the way she pitied even as she died.

---

*Thus ends the galaxy's melodramatic chronicle.*

(Special thanks this episode to the 307th mask of the Aeon of Elation, destroyed after it saw its master cry. Its master said the Aeon of Elation had no need for tears. What the master did not know was that those tears have never stopped.)
